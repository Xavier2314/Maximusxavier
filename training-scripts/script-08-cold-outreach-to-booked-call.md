# Script 08 — Cold Outreach to Booked Discovery Call

**Purpose:** Train team members on how to write professional outreach messages and handle the conversation from first contact through a booked call.  
**Skill Focus:** Cold DM / email etiquette, qualifying without interrogating, handling "what do you charge?" before a call, staying professional without sounding scripted.

---

## Characters

- **Jesse** — Freelancer (you)
- **Brandon** — Prospect, owner of a home services company (plumbing and HVAC)

---

## Part 1: Cold Outreach Message (LinkedIn DM or Email)

---

### Jesse's Opening Message

> Hi Brandon,
>
> I came across your company and noticed you're active on Google — strong reviews and a clear niche in HVAC and plumbing. I specialize in building automations for home services businesses that handle things like lead follow-up, booking confirmations, and review request sequences automatically.
>
> Most of the clients I work with save their office staff 8–12 hours a week once these are set up.
>
> Would it be worth a quick 20-minute call to see if there's a fit? No pitch, just a conversation.
>
> — Jesse

---

### Why This Message Works (Trainer Notes)

- **Personalized** — references Brandon's actual business, not a generic template
- **Specific outcome** — "8–12 hours a week saved" is concrete and relevant to a business owner
- **Low-pressure ask** — "20 minutes" and "no pitch" reduce friction
- **No price mentioned** — price before discovery is a conversation killer

---

## Part 2: Brandon's Reply and Jesse's Response

---

**Brandon:** Hey Jesse, thanks for reaching out. What kind of automations are you talking about exactly? And what does something like that cost?

---

**Jesse's Response:**

> Hey Brandon, glad you replied.
>
> The types of automations I usually build for home services businesses are things like: when a new lead fills out your contact form, they get an immediate text and email response (most leads go with whoever responds first); automated appointment reminders that reduce no-shows; and a post-job review request sequence that goes out automatically 24 hours after a job is marked complete.
>
> On pricing — it genuinely depends on what you have in place already and what you're trying to solve. I've done projects from $400 for a simple single-flow build up to $3,000+ for full CRM + automation setups. The 20-minute call helps me give you a real number instead of a guess.
>
> Are you free sometime this week?

---

### Why This Response Works (Trainer Notes)

- **Answers the "what kind" question** with relevant, concrete examples — not vague descriptions
- **Handles the price question** without dodging it and without giving a number that might scare him off — gives a range and explains why the call matters
- **Ends with a clear CTA** — books the next step

---

## Part 3: The Discovery Call

---

**Jesse:** Hey Brandon, good to finally talk. I appreciate you making time. I'll keep this to 20 minutes like I promised. The goal today is just for me to understand what's going on in your business and be honest with you about whether I can actually help. So let's start — what's a typical day look like for your office team when it comes to handling new leads?

**Brandon:** It's pretty chaotic honestly. We get calls and form fills throughout the day. My office manager tries to call back everyone within a few hours but we miss people, they call competitors, and then sometimes they come back and sometimes they don't.

**Jesse:** How many new leads are you getting per week, roughly?

**Brandon:** Maybe 30 to 40 between the website form and the calls.

**Jesse:** And of those 30 to 40, how many turn into booked jobs?

**Brandon:** Maybe 12 to 15. My wife says we're losing too many but I don't have data to prove where they're going.

**Jesse:** That's a really common situation. Here's what I typically find when I look at this: the leads who don't get a response within the first 5 to 10 minutes after submitting a form have a dramatically lower chance of booking — they've usually already called two other companies. The fix isn't necessarily hiring more staff, it's making the first touchpoint automatic. Do you use any CRM right now, or is it spreadsheets and phone calls?

**Brandon:** We use ServiceTitan for scheduling and invoicing, but leads just... show up in email when someone fills out the website form. Nobody's managing them in a system.

**Jesse:** Okay. ServiceTitan does have some workflow automation built in but it's not always intuitive to set up. There's also a clean option using GoHighLevel as a CRM layer on top — it handles the lead communication side really well, and it can receive form submissions, trigger immediate texts and emails, and track where each lead is in the pipeline. Does your current website form have a way to integrate with external tools?

**Brandon:** I have no idea. My nephew built the site on Wix.

**Jesse:** Wix forms can be connected pretty easily either through Zapier or Make.com. That's not a blocker. Let me ask — what would mean the most to you: more leads responding and converting, fewer no-shows on booked appointments, more reviews, or all three?

**Brandon:** Honestly, step one is stopping the bleeding on the leads. If 30 come in and only 12 book, that's 18 potential jobs we're losing.

**Jesse:** I agree that's the priority. Here's what I'd propose we start with: an automated lead response system — as soon as someone fills out your form, they get a text within 60 seconds and an email, both with your company name, a clear message that says a team member will call within the hour, and ideally a link to see available appointment times. That alone often moves booking rate 20 to 30%. Can I ask — do you have a booking tool, like an online calendar, or does all booking happen over the phone?

**Brandon:** All over the phone right now.

**Jesse:** That's fine for phase one — we focus on the instant response and lead nurture sequence to keep leads warm until your office manager calls. An online booking option is something we can add later. For that first phase, I'd estimate about **$700 to $900** depending on what CRM route we go — I'd scope it fully after this call and send you a proposal. Does that range feel workable?

**Brandon:** I mean, I'd need to understand it better. Can you send me something I can look at?

**Jesse:** Absolutely. I'll send you a proposal by tomorrow — it'll have the workflow mapped out visually so you can see exactly what's automated, what your team still handles, and what each step does. No jargon. I'll also include a second option that layers in the no-show reminder sequence in case you want to do more at once.

**Brandon:** That'd be helpful. Can I share it with my wife? She handles the operations side.

**Jesse:** Of course — I'd actually encourage it. A lot of times the person who deals with the day-to-day operations has questions that help us build something that actually works for your team. If she has questions after reading it, she can email me directly or we can do a quick second call.

**Brandon:** Great. Thanks for being straightforward about this. I've talked to a few tech people and they just go right over my head.

**Jesse:** I appreciate that. My job is to make it simple — you shouldn't need to understand how it works under the hood, just whether it solves the problem. I'll have the proposal to you by end of day tomorrow. Thanks, Brandon.

**Brandon:** Looking forward to it. Thanks Jesse.

## Debrief Notes for Trainees

| Skill | What to Notice |
|---|---|
| **Cold outreach structure** | Short, personalized, one specific outcome, low-pressure ask — never pitch everything in the first message |
| **Price range in DMs** | Giving a range shows transparency and handles the "what do you charge?" without killing the conversation |
| **Discovery questions** | Jesse uses conversion-rate questions ("how many turn into jobs?") to help the client see the problem clearly |
| **Platform awareness** | Jesse knows ServiceTitan, Wix, and GoHighLevel — credibility comes from recognizing the client's tools |
| **Stakeholder inclusion** | Encouraging Brandon to share with his wife is proactive — it reduces the risk of a "I need to check with someone" stall later |
| **Accessible communication** | Brandon's comment at the end is the goal — never make the client feel dumb |

---

## Full Outreach-to-Close Checklist

Use this to track every prospect through the pipeline:

- [ ] First message sent (personalized, short, specific outcome)
- [ ] Follow-up sent if no reply in 3 days (one follow-up only)
- [ ] Discovery call booked
- [ ] Discovery call completed — pain points documented
- [ ] Proposal sent within 24 hours of call
- [ ] Proposal follow-up sent if no reply in 3 days
- [ ] Contract and deposit received
- [ ] Project kickoff scheduled

## Practice Exercise

Write your own cold outreach message for a prospect who:
- Owns a law firm
- Has a website on Squarespace
- Gets leads from Google but has no follow-up system

Exchange messages with a partner and give each other feedback on: specificity, tone, the CTA, and whether the message sounds human or templated.
