# Script 09 — Facebook Gig Link Inquiry: Full Conversation That Doesn't Close

**Purpose:** Train team members on handling a full prospect journey — from a cold Facebook DM all the way through a discovery call — where the deal does NOT close due to pricing or timeline. The goal is to stay professional, leave the door open, and protect your positioning.  
**Skill Focus:** Warm DM responses, pre-call qualifying, full discovery, handling price shock, handling timeline objections, graceful exit without desperation.

---

## Characters

- **You (Alex)** — Freelancer on your team
- **Marcus** — Prospect, owns a small landscaping and lawn care company with 4 employees

---

## Platform

- Stage 1–2: Facebook Messenger (DM)
- Stage 3–5: Phone / Video Discovery Call

---

# STAGE 1 — The Facebook DM

---

**[Marcus — Facebook DM]**
Hey I saw your post about automation, can you inform me on the automation you are trying to build or create

---

**[Alex]**
Hey Marcus! Thanks for reaching out — glad the post caught your eye.

So the automations I build are basically systems that handle repetitive tasks in your business automatically — things like following up with new leads, sending appointment reminders, notifying your team when something needs attention, or connecting tools you already use so data moves between them without anyone doing it manually.

The idea is that instead of you or your team doing the same tasks over and over, a workflow runs in the background and handles it for you.

What kind of business do you run? That'll help me give you a more specific example of what it could look like for you.

---

**[Marcus]**
I run a landscaping company. Me and 3 other guys. We do lawn care, cleanups, that kind of thing

---

**[Alex]**
That's a great fit actually. A lot of service businesses like yours spend way too much time on stuff like sending quote follow-ups, confirming appointments, or chasing down reviews — and that's exactly the kind of thing automation handles really well.

Quick example: right now, when someone reaches out for a quote, what happens? Do you call them back yourself, does someone text them, or do they just have to wait?

---

**[Marcus]**
Mostly I call them back when I get a chance. Sometimes it takes a day or two if we're out on jobs

---

**[Alex]**
Yeah that's super common with field service businesses. The problem is most people reach out to 2 or 3 companies at the same time, and whoever responds first usually gets the job.

So if it's taking a day or two, you might be losing jobs without even knowing it — not because of your prices or your work, but just because someone else replied faster.

That's honestly one of the first things I'd fix for a business like yours.

---

**[Marcus]**
Wow I never really thought about it like that. So what would that look like

---

**[Alex]**
So basically the moment someone fills out a contact form on your website, or sends you a Facebook message, or texts your business number — the system sends them an automatic reply right away. Like within 60 seconds.

Something simple like: "Hey, this is Marcus from [your company name], thanks for reaching out! We'll have someone call you within the hour to go over your quote. In the meantime, here's what we offer: [link]."

They feel taken care of immediately. You don't have to stop what you're doing on the job. And by the time you do call them, they already feel like they've heard from you.

---

**[Marcus]**
That would actually be really helpful. We lose a lot of potential customers I think

---

**[Alex]**
I believe it. And the good news is it's very fixable — this isn't complicated technology, it just needs to be set up properly.

Are you open to jumping on a quick call? Like 20 minutes, I can ask you a few questions about how your business works right now and tell you honestly what I'd recommend and what it would cost. No pressure to do anything on the call.

---

**[Marcus]**
Yeah sure. What days work for you

I'm flexible this week. I can do Wednesday or Thursday, morning or afternoon — whatever works around your job schedule. What's better for you?

**[Marcus]**

---

**[Alex]**
Thursday at 3 PM works perfectly. I'll send you a quick reminder the morning of. 

One last thing before the call — do you have a website already, or are people mostly finding you through Facebook and word of mouth?

**[Marcus]**

---

**[Alex]**
Perfect, that gives us something to work with. I'll take a quick look at both before Thursday so I come prepared.

**[Marcus]**

# STAGE 3 — The Discovery Call

*[3:00 PM — Phone call begins]*

---

**[Alex]**
Hey Marcus, Alex here. Can you hear me okay?

---

**[Marcus]**
Yeah I got you. Just got done with a job, good timing.

---

**[Alex]**
Perfect. I'll keep this to 20 minutes like I said. The goal today is really just for me to understand how your business works right now and then I'll be straight with you about whether I think automation would actually make a difference for you — and what it would cost. Cool?

---

**[Marcus]**
Yeah that works for me.

---

**[Alex]**
Alright. So let's start from the top. Walk me through what happens right now from the moment a new customer reaches out to you — like, all the steps until they're booked on your schedule.

---

**[Marcus]**
So usually they either call me directly, text me, or message me on Facebook. Sometimes they fill out the thing on my website. I try to get back to them same day but like I said, when we're on jobs it can take a while. If I can I'll call them back, we talk about what they need, I give them a rough price, and then if they want to move forward I put them in my schedule. I use Google Calendar for that.

---

**[Alex]**
Okay good. And when you say you give them a rough price on the call — do you ever send them a written quote? Like an email or a text with the breakdown?

---

**[Marcus]**
Not really. I just kind of tell them verbally. Sometimes I'll write it in a text but it's not like a formal thing.

---

**[Alex]**
Got it. After that call — if they say "let me think about it" or "I'll get back to you" — do you follow up with them? Or do you kind of wait and see if they come back?

---

**[Marcus]**
I'll maybe text them once. But honestly if they don't reply I usually just move on. I don't want to be annoying.

---

**[Alex]**
That's really common. Most service business owners feel the same way — they don't want to seem pushy. But here's the thing: research actually shows that most people need 3 to 5 follow-ups before they make a decision. One text and done means you're leaving a lot of potential jobs on the table.

How many new leads would you say reach out to you in a typical week?

---

**[Marcus]**
Maybe 8 to 12. Sometimes more if I've been posting on Facebook.

---

**[Alex]**
And out of those 8 to 12, how many actually become paying jobs?

---

**[Marcus]**
Honestly probably like 3 or 4. Maybe 5 on a good week.

---

**[Alex]**
So you're converting roughly a third. That means 5 to 8 people a week are reaching out and not booking. Some of those are just price shoppers, that's normal. But I'd bet a meaningful chunk of them just didn't hear back fast enough or didn't get followed up with.

If you booked even 2 extra jobs a week at your average job rate — what would that add up to monthly?

---

**[Marcus]**
I mean a basic lawn job is like $80 to $120. Bigger cleanups can be $300 or more. So I don't know, maybe an extra $600 to $800 a month? Probably more in spring when it's busy.

---

**[Alex]**
Right. So we're talking potentially $600 to $1,000 in additional revenue per month just from responding faster and following up better. And we haven't even talked about the time you'd save on the back-and-forth.

Alright, let me ask a couple more things. When you book a job — do you send them any kind of confirmation? Like "your appointment is set for Thursday at 10 AM"?

---

**[Marcus]**
Sometimes I'll text them. But I forget sometimes. And then they forget and I show up and nobody's home. That's happened a few times.

---

**[Alex]**
Yeah, no-shows are one of the biggest wastes of time for field service businesses. You drove out there, blocked that time, maybe turned down another job. 

What about after a job is done — do you have a way of asking customers for a Google review?

---

**[Marcus]**
I ask sometimes in person but most people don't do it. I only have like 7 reviews on Google right now.

---

**[Alex]**
Seven reviews. And how many jobs have you done total, roughly?

---

**[Marcus]**
I've been doing this for about 4 years. So probably a few hundred at least.

---

**[Alex]**
So you've done hundreds of jobs and only 7 reviews. That's a huge missed opportunity — not because the reviews don't come in, but because nobody's asking in the right way at the right time. Most people will leave a review if they get a text with a direct link within an hour of the job being done. If they have to Google your business later and find the link themselves, they just don't do it.

---

**[Marcus]**
Man. I knew I needed to do something about that. My competitor across town has like 80 reviews and I know his work isn't better than mine.

---

**[Alex]**
I believe you. And this is all fixable. Let me tell you what I'm thinking in terms of a solution, and then you can tell me if it makes sense.

---

**[Marcus]**
Yeah go ahead.

---

**[Alex]**
So I'd build you three connected automations. Think of them as three problems solved with one system.

The first one is the **Lead Response Automation**. The moment someone fills out your website form, messages your Facebook page, or texts a number we set up for your business — they get an automatic reply within 60 seconds. It confirms you got their message, tells them someone will call them shortly, and keeps them warm so they don't go with someone else while you're finishing a job.

The second is the **Appointment Confirmation and Reminder**. When a job gets booked, the customer automatically gets a confirmation text or email. Then 24 hours before the job, they get a reminder. No-shows drop significantly with this in place.

The third is the **Review Request Sequence**. About an hour after a job is marked as complete, the customer gets a text with a direct link to your Google review page. Simple message, one tap. Over time this builds your review count consistently without you having to remember to ask every time.

All three run automatically in the background. You don't change how you work — the system just handles the follow-through that currently falls through the cracks.

---

**[Marcus]**
Okay that all sounds really good. Like, all three of those are problems I have. What does something like that cost?

---

**[Alex]**
So for all three automations built and tested, connected to your website, Facebook page, and Google Calendar — my price for that full setup is **$1,200**.

---

*[Silence for a moment]*

---

**[Marcus]**

---

**[Alex]**
What were you expecting, if you don't mind me asking? I just want to make sure I understand where you're coming from.

---

**[Marcus]**
I don't know, I guess I was thinking like $200 or $300 maybe. I didn't really know what to expect but $1,200 feels like a lot for a small business like mine.

---

**[Alex]**
That's fair and I appreciate you being straight with me. Can I explain what's in that number so you can decide if it makes sense for where you're at?

---

**[Marcus]**
Yeah go ahead.

---

**[Alex]**
The $1,200 covers three things. First, the actual build — I'm setting up the automation software, connecting it to your website form, your Facebook messages, your Google Calendar, your texting number, and Google reviews. Each of those connections has to be configured and tested. Second, I'm setting up a CRM — a simple lead tracking system — so you can see every lead that came in, when they were contacted, and whether they booked or not. Right now you have no visibility into that. Third, I include a two-week support period after delivery where I fix anything that isn't working the way it should.

The reason I don't do $200 builds is because at that price you're getting something that works once, breaks when Facebook updates their API, and has no error handling. If a lead falls through a crack in a cheap automation, you don't even know it happened. That's worse than doing it manually.

---

**[Marcus]**
No I get that. I just — $1,200 is a big number for me right now. We just bought a new trailer last month and cash is tight.

---

**[Alex]**
I hear you, and I respect that. I'm not going to try to talk you into something that puts you in a tough spot financially.

Let me offer you a couple of honest options so you can decide what makes the most sense.

Option one — we do the full package at $1,200, but I split it into two payments. $600 upfront to start, $600 when I deliver and everything is working. That way you're not hitting $1,200 all at once.

Option two — we start smaller. Just the lead response automation — the instant reply when someone reaches out. That's the highest impact piece and the fastest return. I'd do just that one for $450. It doesn't include the CRM, the reminders, or the review requests, but it fixes the biggest leak first. Later, when cash flow is better, we can add the rest.

Which of those feels more realistic for you?

---

**[Marcus]**
Honestly even $450 feels tight right now. Like I said we're in a slow period. Spring is when things really pick up for us.

---

**[Alex]**
Okay, I appreciate you being honest with me. I'd rather know that now than have you agree to something and then have it be stressful.

Can I ask — when does your spring season typically start? Like when do you start seeing volume pick back up?

---

**[Marcus]**
Usually mid-March. That's when we get slammed. We can barely keep up honestly.

---

**[Alex]**
So you've got about two months until things get busy. Here's the thing about automation — the best time to set it up is *before* the rush, not during it. When you're slammed in March and April, you won't have time to onboard something new. And every day in the busy season without the lead response system is a day you might be losing jobs to faster competitors.

I'm not saying that to pressure you. I'm saying it because it's the reality of when this is most useful to you.

---

**[Marcus]**
I know, you're right. But I just can't swing it right now. Is there any way to do like a payment plan over a few months?

---

**[Alex]**
I understand where you're coming from, and I respect that. I don't do extended payment plans personally — I've had situations in the past where it creates problems on both sides. But here's what I can do.

I can hold your spot and give you the same pricing if you want to come back in 4 to 6 weeks. Prices for this kind of work do go up over time as demand grows, but I'm happy to honor today's quote for you until the end of February. That gives you time to get through this slow stretch and then pull the trigger before your busy season hits.

---

**[Marcus]**
That's actually really fair. I appreciate that.

---

**[Alex]**
Of course. And honestly Marcus, everything we talked about today is still true in February. The leads, the no-shows, the review gap — none of that goes away on its own. When you're ready, the solution is ready too.

---

**[Marcus]**
Yeah. I feel like I need this, I just need to time it right.

---

**[Alex]**
That makes total sense. Here's what I'll do — I'll send you a quick summary of everything we talked about today so you have it in writing. The three automations, what's included, the pricing options. That way when you're ready to move forward you don't have to start from scratch and we can just pick back up.

Smart move — having her on the same page makes the decision easier. I'll write it up in plain language, not techy stuff. Should have it to you by tonight or tomorrow morning.

That's how I prefer to work — I'd rather you fully understand what you're getting than just say yes without knowing what you bought. I'll be in touch.

One last thing — is it okay if I follow up with you once in about three to four weeks just to check in and see where things are? No pressure, just a quick message.

**[Marcus]**

## Debrief Notes for Trainees

| Stage | Skill | What to Notice |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook DM** | Warm opener + qualify immediately | Alex doesn't pitch — he asks about Marcus's business first. The prospect feels heard, not sold to |
| **Pre-call research** | Shows up prepared | Alex reviewed the website and Facebook page before the call — this builds instant credibility |
| **Discovery call opening** | Setting expectations | Alex reminds Marcus of the 20-minute promise AND says "no pressure" — lowers guard immediately |
| **Current state questions** | Layered discovery | Each question builds on the last answer — Alex never jumps ahead to the solution until he has full picture |
| **Revenue math** | Making the pain real | "2 extra jobs a week = $600–$800/month" — Alex helps Marcus quantify the problem, not just describe it |
| **Solution presentation** | Three-part structure | Lead response → appointment reminders → review requests. Named clearly, explained in plain language |
| **Price delivery** | Confident, not apologetic | Alex states "$1,200" clearly and then waits. He doesn't hedge or immediately justify |
| **Price shock response** | Don't panic, ask first | When Marcus is surprised, Alex asks "what were you expecting?" before defending — this is critical |
| **Objection handling** | Two honest options | Alex offers a payment split AND a smaller scope — he doesn't just discount. He gives real choices |
| **Timeline objection** | Plant the spring seed | Alex ties the value to Marcus's peak season — not manipulative, just honest and relevant |
| **Not closing** | Graceful exit | Alex doesn't beg, doesn't drop the price further, doesn't make Marcus feel bad. He provides a written summary and a locked price |
| **Follow-up ask** | Permission-based** | Alex asks if it's okay to follow up — Marcus says yes. This makes the follow-up message feel welcome, not spam |
| **Follow-up message** | Short, no pressure | Three weeks later, one short message. It works because trust was already built |

---

## What Alex Did NOT Do (Just As Important)

- Did NOT drop the price below his floor when pushed
- Did NOT say "I'll make an exception for you this time"
- Did NOT send multiple follow-up messages when no reply came
- Did NOT end the call with vague language like "think about it and let me know"
- Did NOT make Marcus feel guilty for not being ready to buy
- Did NOT oversell or promise results he couldn't guarantee

---

## Key Lesson for Trainees

**Not every call closes, and that's okay.** A conversation that ends without a sale is not a failure — it's a relationship. Marcus now knows who Alex is, trusts him, and has already agreed to talk again. That follow-up call three weeks later has a much higher chance of closing than a cold outreach ever would.

The professional difference between a freelancer and an amateur is what happens when the client says no. Amateurs either beg and discount or disappear. Professionals leave a door open and walk through it with patience.

## Practice Exercise

Role-play this full script end to end with a partner. It should take about 20 to 25 minutes.

Variations to try:
1. The client says the timeline (5–7 days) is the problem, not the price — their event is in 48 hours. Practice navigating this version.
2. The client says "let me think about it" and goes quiet. Practice writing the follow-up message for 3 days later — short, warm, not desperate.
3. The client counters with "My friend said he'd do it for $300." Practice explaining the difference without attacking the competitor.
