# Script 10 — Facebook Gig Inquiry: Salon Owner Who Needs to Check With Her Partner

**Purpose:** Train team members on handling a full prospect journey — from a Facebook DM through a detailed discovery call — where the deal stalls because the client needs to check with a business partner and then goes quiet. This script teaches how to keep momentum without being pushy, how to handle the "I need to talk to my partner" objection professionally, and how to follow up through silence.  
**Skill Focus:** Building rapport over DM, full discovery for a service business, handling the "let me check with someone" stall, multi-message follow-up strategy, knowing when to move on.

---

## Characters

- **You (Jamie)** — Freelancer on your team
- **Tanya** — Prospect, co-owns a hair salon with her sister Brianna. Tanya handles most of the client-facing side; Brianna handles finances and business decisions.

---

## Platform

- Stage 1–2: Facebook Messenger
- Stage 3–5: Phone / Video Discovery Call
- Stage 6: Follow-up messages (Facebook DM + text)

## Debrief Notes for Trainees

| Stage | Skill | What to Notice |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening DM** | Warmth + immediate qualify | Jamie matches Tanya's energy (friendly, enthusiastic) while still gathering business info — never skips the qualifying questions |
| **Instagram research** | Personalized compliment | Mentioning the salon's Instagram content before the call makes Tanya feel seen, not just sold to |
| **Call opening** | Set expectations + price promise | Jamie says "I'll be straight about cost" upfront — Tanya literally thanks her for it. This is a huge trust-builder |
| **Discovery flow** | Current state → numbers → pain** | Jamie asks volume (15-20 DMs/week), conversion (8-10 booked), and then does the revenue math out loud with Tanya so SHE arrives at the impact, not Jamie |
| **Three-part solution** | Named, plain-language, sequenced | Each automation is named and described in terms of what the client experiences, not what the tech does |
| **"I need to talk to my sister"** | Don't try to close around it | Jamie does NOT say "what would it take to get your yes today?" She validates it, asks about Brianna's likely concerns, and offers a joint call |
| **Written summary** | Designed to be shared | Jamie writes the summary knowing Brianna will read it — plain language, no jargon, clear price and timeline |
| **The silence** | Three-message follow-up strategy | Friday check-in (light), Tuesday follow-up (door open, 30-day price lock), final Monday message (no pressure, genuine warmth). Three messages, then stop |
| **Tanya comes back** | The reply reveals the real objection | It wasn't that Brianna said no — it was timing and cash flow. This is why you follow up rather than assume rejection |
| **Pre-summer follow-up** | Timed to their business cycle | Jamie noted the timeline and followed up at exactly the right moment — Tanya and Brianna were already talking about it |

## The Three-Message Follow-Up Rule

When a prospect goes quiet after saying "I'll check with someone," follow this sequence:

| Message | Timing | Tone | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Message 1** | 2 to 3 days after the call | Light check-in, friendly | See if they had the conversation |
| **Message 2** | 7 days after the call | Warm, door-open, price note | Show patience, maintain value |
| **Message 3** | 14 days after the call | Closing the loop, genuine | Let them know you're moving on but the door stays open |

After three messages with no reply — **stop**. You've done your job. If they come back later (like Tanya did), respond warmly with zero guilt-tripping.

## What Jamie Did NOT Do

- Did NOT try to close Tanya without Brianna involved
- Did NOT say "Can't you just decide on your own?"
- Did NOT send five follow-up messages in a week
- Did NOT guilt-trip Tanya when she finally replied after 16 days
- Did NOT lose momentum when Brianna said wait — she immediately connected the timing to summer strategy
- Did NOT make Tanya feel bad for going quiet

---

## The Key Lesson — "I Need to Check With Someone" Is Not a No

This objection is one of the most common stalls in freelance sales. The mistake most beginners make is treating it as a soft rejection and either pushing harder or giving up. The professional response is:

1. **Validate it** — "That makes complete sense"
2. **Understand it** — Ask what questions the other person might have
3. **Arm them** — Give them a clear document to share so they can advocate for you when you're not in the room
4. **Offer access** — Invite the other decision-maker onto a call
5. **Follow up with patience** — Three messages, then give space

The sale that takes six weeks to close is still a sale. The client who feels respected during the wait becomes a long-term client.

## Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1 — The Joint Call**
Role-play the scenario where Tanya replies: "My sister wants to hop on a quick call with you. Can you do Friday?" Practice running a discovery-to-close call with two stakeholders at once — one enthusiastic (Tanya) and one skeptical (Brianna). How do you address Brianna's doubts without making Tanya feel like she wasn't enough to make the decision?

**Exercise 2 — Write the Three Follow-Up Messages**
Without looking at the script, write your own versions of the three follow-up messages Jamie sent. Compare them to the originals and ask: Are they warm or cold? Do they maintain value or start discounting? Do they pressure or invite?

**Exercise 3 — Handle the Price Question From Brianna**
Brianna joins a call and says: "Tanya showed me the proposal. Honestly $1,400 feels like a lot for a small salon. Can you do $900?" Role-play Jamie's response — hold the price, explain the value, and offer a reduced-scope option without losing the relationship.
