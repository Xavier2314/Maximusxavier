# Script 02 — Client Landing Page Project: Understanding Requirements and Proposing a Solution

**Scenario:** A freelancer is on a discovery call with a coach who needs a landing page to capture leads for a new online program. The freelancer must uncover the real goal, understand the audience, clarify technical requirements, and propose a complete solution.

**Platforms Featured:** Wix, GoHighLevel, Make.com, Mailchimp, Calendly

---

## PART 1 — OPENING

**Freelancer (Marcus):** Hey James, thanks for jumping on the call. I saw your intake form and it looks like you're launching something new — a coaching program. That's exciting. Tell me about it.

**Client (James):** Yeah, I've been doing one-on-one coaching for about three years now, and I finally want to launch a group program. I need a landing page to start collecting interest and eventually sign people up.

**Marcus:** Love it. So this is a launch — not just a static page, you need it to actually do something for you in terms of capturing leads and moving people into the program.

**James:** Exactly. I want people to come to the page, read about what I'm offering, and then either book a call with me or sign up for a waitlist.

---

## PART 2 — REQUIREMENT GATHERING

**Marcus:** Okay, let's make sure I understand all the pieces. First — what platform is your current website on? Do you have a website already?

**James:** I have a Wix website. It's basic but it works. I'd want the landing page to match the look and feel of what I already have.

**Marcus:** Got it — so we're building within Wix, keeping it consistent with your existing brand. What's the program called, and do you have copy written for it — like the headline, the offer details, the bullet points?

**James:** I have a rough draft. It's not polished but the bones are there.

**Marcus:** That's fine. I can work with rough copy and help clean it up. Do you have photos or a headshot we can use?

**James:** Yes, I had a photoshoot done last month. I can send you those.

**Marcus:** Great. Now let's talk about what happens after someone fills out the form. When a person submits their name and email, what should happen next?

**James:** I want them to get an email confirmation right away. And I want to be notified so I can follow up personally.

**Marcus:** Should they be added to a specific email list? Are you using any email marketing software right now?

**James:** I use Mailchimp but I haven't really set up any automation there.

**Marcus:** Okay, so we'd want the form submission to automatically add them to a Mailchimp list and trigger a confirmation email. What about booking calls — you mentioned Calendly?

**James:** Yeah I use Calendly for all my calls. I'd want a button on the page that takes people straight to my booking link.

**Marcus:** Perfect. And the waitlist — is that separate from booking a call, or is the waitlist the same as the call?

**James:** Good question. I want two options: one button to book a discovery call with me, and one to just join the waitlist if they're not ready to book.

**Marcus:** So two conversion paths — a direct booking path and a softer "join the waitlist" path. That's a smart way to capture people at different stages of readiness. Got it.

---

## PART 3 — CLARIFYING GOALS AND SUCCESS METRICS

**Marcus:** Let me ask you something a lot of people skip over — how will you know this landing page is working? What does success look like for you in the first 30 days?

**James:** Honestly, if I can get 50 people on the waitlist and book 10 discovery calls, I'd consider that a great start.

**Marcus:** That's helpful. Knowing those targets helps me think about how we structure the page — things like where the CTAs go, how many times we repeat the offer, whether we use urgency messaging. All of that affects conversion.

**James:** I hadn't thought about it that way. I just assumed a page is a page.

**Marcus:** A page is a tool. How it's built determines whether it works or not. There's a big difference between a landing page that gets 2% of visitors to take action versus one that gets 12%.

---

## PART 4 — SOLUTION PRESENTATION

**Marcus:** Here's what I'd propose for you. We build a focused, high-converting landing page on Wix. The page structure would be: a strong headline and subheadline, a short intro video or photo section, a breakdown of what's included in the program, social proof — if you have testimonials — and two clear CTAs: book a call, or join the waitlist.

Behind the page, I'd connect everything using Make.com. So when someone joins the waitlist, Make.com automatically adds them to a Mailchimp list, sends them a branded confirmation email, and sends you a Slack or email notification so you know someone signed up in real-time.

For the booking path, the Calendly embed handles everything — someone clicks the button, books a slot, and your calendar handles the rest.

**James:** That sounds really thorough. What about the design — will it look professional?

**Marcus:** Yes. I'll match the fonts, colors, and overall feel of your existing Wix site. I'll also make sure it's mobile-optimized because the majority of people will be landing on this from Instagram or Facebook on their phones.

**James:** Oh, good point. I didn't even think about mobile.

---

## PART 5 — HANDLING OBJECTIONS

**James:** How long would this take? I want to launch in two weeks.

**Marcus:** Two weeks is doable if we move quickly on the content side. The biggest thing that slows down landing page projects is waiting on copy and photos from the client. If you can get me the rough copy and photos within 48 hours of us kicking off, I can have a first draft ready for review by day 5, final version by day 8 or 9, and we'd have a few days buffer before your launch.

**James:** That works. I can do that.

**Marcus:** And I should mention — once we go live, I'd recommend doing a quick A/B test between two headline variations to see which one converts better. That's something we can talk about after launch.

**James:** I don't even know what that means but I like the sound of it.

**Marcus:** I'll explain it properly in the proposal. It's basically a low-effort way to improve your results after launch.

**James:** What's the investment on something like this?

**Marcus:** For the full package — Wix landing page, two CTA paths, Make.com integration connecting Mailchimp and your notification system, mobile optimization, and a 2-week post-launch support window — I'd quote this at $950 to $1,300. Where it lands depends on how much of the copy you want me to refine versus just format. Do you want me to build in copywriting as part of the scope?

**James:** Maybe light editing rather than full copywriting.

**Marcus:** Then we're looking at the lower end of that range — probably $950 flat.

---

## PART 6 — CLOSING

**Marcus:** Here's what I'd suggest as next steps: I'll send you a short proposal tonight that outlines exactly what we discussed — the page structure, the integrations, the timeline, and the investment. If it all looks good, we sign off and you send me the copy and photos. We'd kick off immediately after that.

**James:** That sounds great. Should I send you anything before the proposal?

**Marcus:** If you want to share a link to your existing Wix site so I can get a feel for your brand, that would be helpful. And if you have a competitor or someone whose landing page you really like, send that too — it helps me understand your taste.

**James:** I can do that right now, actually.

**Marcus:** Perfect. I'll take a look before I write the proposal. Talk soon, James.

---

## KEY LESSONS FOR TEAM MEMBERS

| Skill | What to Notice in This Script |
|---|---|
| **Clarifying "what happens next"** | Marcus always asks what should happen after a form is submitted — don't assume |
| **Two conversion paths** | Great freelancers identify when a client needs multiple CTAs for different buyer stages |
| **Copy and asset dependencies** | Set expectations early about what the client needs to provide and when |
| **Success metrics** | Ask the client to define success — it shows professionalism and helps scope the project |
| **Teaching the client** | Marcus explains things like A/B testing without being condescending — this builds authority |
| **Mobile-first reminder** | Always bring up mobile optimization — most clients forget and it affects conversions |
| **Scoping copywriting separately** | Copywriting is a separate service — clarify upfront how much you're providing |
