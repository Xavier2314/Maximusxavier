# Script 01 — Client Automation Project: Taking a Client from Point A to Point B

**Scenario:** A freelancer gets on a discovery call with a small business owner who is doing everything manually and wants to automate their workflow. The freelancer's job is to understand the current process (Point A), define the desired outcome (Point B), and propose an automation solution.

**Platforms Featured:** Make.com, n8n, Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack

---

## PART 1 — OPENING & RAPPORT BUILDING

**Freelancer (Alex):** Hi Sarah, thanks for making time today. I'm really looking forward to learning more about your business and where you want to take things. Before we dive in, how's your week going?

**Client (Sarah):** Honestly, it's been a bit hectic. We have a lot going on and I feel like I'm constantly putting out fires instead of actually growing the business.

**Alex:** I hear that a lot from business owners at your stage. That feeling of being reactive instead of proactive — it's really common, and honestly it's often a sign that you've outgrown your current processes. Which is actually a good problem to have. So let's talk about what that looks like for you specifically.

---

## PART 2 — DISCOVERY: UNDERSTANDING POINT A (THE CURRENT STATE)

**Alex:** Can you walk me through a typical day or week for your team? I want to understand how work currently flows through your business.

**Sarah:** Sure. So when a new lead comes in — whether it's from our website form, Instagram, or referrals — someone on my team has to manually copy the information into a spreadsheet. Then we send a welcome email, schedule a call, and follow up a few days later. It's all done by hand.

**Alex:** Okay, so right now every lead requires manual data entry, manual email sending, and manual follow-up scheduling?

**Sarah:** Exactly. And half the time things fall through the cracks because we're busy with other clients.

**Alex:** How many leads are you getting per week on average?

**Sarah:** Maybe 20 to 30.

**Alex:** And how many people are handling this process?

**Sarah:** Just two of us, and we both have other responsibilities.

**Alex:** I see. So two people are spending time on repetitive manual tasks that could potentially be automated — and because of the manual nature, leads are occasionally being missed or delayed.

**Sarah:** Yes, that's exactly it.

**Alex:** One more question on the current state — where does the lead information actually live right now? Is it one spreadsheet, multiple systems?

**Sarah:** We have a Google Sheet, but honestly it's a mess. Some things get logged, some don't. It's not consistent.

**Alex:** Perfect — that's really helpful context. Now let me ask you about where you want to be.

---

## PART 3 — DISCOVERY: DEFINING POINT B (THE DESIRED OUTCOME)

**Alex:** If we could wave a magic wand and fix this process, what would the ideal version look like to you?

**Sarah:** I'd want every lead to automatically get logged. And I'd love for them to automatically receive a welcome email right away — not hours later when someone gets around to it.

**Alex:** That makes total sense. What about follow-up? If someone doesn't respond to the welcome email, what should happen?

**Sarah:** They should get a follow-up automatically, maybe 2 days later. And then if still no response, maybe one more after that.

**Alex:** So you're describing a 3-touch automated sequence. Got it. What about your team — should they be notified when a new lead comes in?

**Sarah:** Yes! Right now we don't always know a lead came in until we check the sheet. It would be amazing to get a Slack message or something when a new lead arrives.

**Alex:** So to summarize Point B: every lead from every source gets auto-logged into a clean system, receives an immediate welcome email, gets a 2-day follow-up if no response, and your team gets a real-time Slack notification for each new lead. Does that capture it?

**Sarah:** That's exactly what I want.

---

## PART 4 — SOLUTION PRESENTATION

**Alex:** Great. What I'm going to propose is a fully automated lead management workflow. Here's how it would work at a high level.

When someone fills out your website form, or sends a message on Instagram, or gets referred — that information flows automatically into a clean, organized Google Sheet or a CRM. At the same time, two things happen in parallel: one, your team gets a Slack notification with the lead's details; and two, the lead receives a personalized welcome email within seconds.

If the lead doesn't respond within 48 hours, the system automatically sends a follow-up. If there's still no response after another 48 hours, it sends a final check-in. After that, the lead gets tagged as "cold" and moves to a nurture sequence.

**Sarah:** That sounds amazing. What tools would you use to build this?

**Alex:** For the automation engine, I'd use Make.com — it's a visual automation platform that connects all your apps together. We'd connect your website form, your Google Sheet or CRM, Gmail for the emails, and Slack. The whole flow would run 24/7 without anyone needing to touch it.

**Sarah:** How long would that take to build?

**Alex:** A workflow like this typically takes 5 to 7 business days from kickoff to delivery, including testing. I'd also include a walkthrough so your team knows how it works and what to do if anything needs to be adjusted.

---

## PART 5 — HANDLING OBJECTIONS

**Sarah:** This sounds great, but honestly I'm a little worried about the cost. We're a small team.

**Alex:** That's a fair concern, and I appreciate you being upfront about it. Let me reframe it this way: right now, two people are spending time every day on manual data entry, manual emails, and follow-up tracking. That time has a cost — either in hours, in missed leads, or in the mental load of trying to keep track of everything. What I'm building eliminates that cost and adds speed and consistency that most small businesses can't get any other way. The investment typically pays for itself within the first month just from leads that would have otherwise been missed.

**Sarah:** That makes sense. What does it cost?

**Alex:** For a workflow like this — covering three lead sources, the welcome sequence, the follow-up sequence, and the Slack integration — I'd quote this at $800 to $1,200 depending on the complexity of your form fields and how many email templates we build. That's a one-time build fee. Make.com itself costs around $9/month on their basic plan, which you'd manage directly.

**Sarah:** Okay, that's more reasonable than I expected.

**Alex:** I also offer a 30-day support window after delivery, so if anything needs tweaking or a new lead source gets added, I've got you covered.

---

## PART 6 — CLOSING

**Alex:** So here's what I'd suggest as a next step: I'll put together a simple one-page proposal that outlines exactly what we discussed — the workflow, the tools, the timeline, and the investment. You'd review it, we do a quick 15-minute call to answer any questions, and if it all looks good, we kick off. How does that sound?

**Sarah:** That sounds really good. Yeah, let's do that.

**Alex:** Perfect. I'll have that over to you within 24 hours. One last thing — is there anyone else on your team who should be part of the kickoff conversation, or are you the main decision-maker?

**Sarah:** It's just me for decisions. My assistant would need to be looped in once we start.

**Alex:** Got it. I'll address the proposal to you and CC your assistant once we're in the onboarding phase. Looking forward to it, Sarah.

## KEY LESSONS FOR TEAM MEMBERS

| Skill | What to Notice in This Script |
|---|---|
| **Rapport building** | Alex opens with a casual question to lower the client's guard before asking business questions |
| **Discovery questions** | Alex asks open-ended questions and lets the client describe the pain in their own words |
| **Reflecting back** | Alex summarizes the client's pain points before proposing a solution — this builds trust |
| **Point A / Point B framing** | Never propose a solution until you fully understand the current state AND the desired state |
| **Handling price objections** | Reframe cost as ROI, not as an expense — lead with value before revealing the number |
| **Closing the call** | Always end with a clear, specific next step — never leave the call open-ended |
| **Decision-maker check** | Always ask who else is involved in the decision before writing a proposal |
